A Defense Cooperation Agreement (DCA) is a formal [[Bilateral Alliances|bilateral]] or [[Multilateral Military Alliances|multilateral]] arrangement between two or more countries outlining the terms and framework for military collaboration. These agreements typically do not constitute full defense alliances or mutual defense pacts but instead facilitate a broad range of peacetime military activities, including:
- ***Non-binding mutual commitments***: While DCA's establish a formal relationship, they rarely involve treaty-level obligations like mutual defense clauses. 
- ***Scope of Cooperation***:
	- Joint Military Exercises
	- Intelligence Sharing and Consultations
	- Access to Military Facilities or Ports
	- Defense Technology Exchange
	- Arms Sales or Logistics Support
	- Humanitarian Assistance and Disaster Relief (HADR)
- ***Legal and Operational Framework***: DCAs often specify [[Status of Forces Agreements (SOFAs)]] or related protocols governing the legal status of visiting forces, jurisdiction, and liability. 
- ***Strategic Signaling***: While practical in nature, DCAs often serve diplomatic or geopolitical purposes--signaling deepening ties or shared security interests without invoking alliance-level obligations. 

DCAs are thus a flexible, politically significant tool of international security engagement—used especially in regions like [[Southeast Asia]] where formal alliances are rare and strategic hedging is the norm.
### **Examples**
- [[Singapore-Australia DCA (2020)]]: Allows for long-term training of Singaporean troops in northern Australia and expanded defense technology collaboration.
- [[US-Vietnam DCA (2011)]]: Laid the groundwork for a growing strategic partnership, enabling naval port visits and increased defense dialogue.
- [[India-Philippines DCA (2022)]]: Includes training, capacity-building, and maritime cooperation, amid rising tensions in the South China Sea.
