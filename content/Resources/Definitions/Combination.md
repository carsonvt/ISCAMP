During World War II, “combination” referred to the integrated military, logistical, and production efforts of the Allies—especially Britain and the U.S.—through [[United Nations High Command|intergovernmental boards]], commands, and planning structures.

The term was adapted from Anglo-French cooperation efforts in WWI and redefined by the Anglo-American [[Combined Chiefs of Staff (CCS)]] at the [[Arcadia Conference]] (Dec 1941–Jan 1942). From this point forward, “[[Combined Operations|combined]]” referred exclusively to international (i.e., multi-nation) cooperation, while “[[Jointness|joint]]” referred to inter-service cooperation within a single nation.

_Related Terms_: [[Jointness|Joint]], inter-Allied cooperation, [[Interoperability|interoperability]].
