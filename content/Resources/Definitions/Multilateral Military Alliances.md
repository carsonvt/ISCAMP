A multilateral military alliance is a formal defense partnership involving three or more countries that commit to collective security and coordinated military cooperation. 

Unlike [[Bilateral Alliances|bilateral alliances]], which involve just two nations, multilateral alliances are built on shared [[Military Interests|interests]] across a broader coalition of states. These alliances often include joint command structures, standardized operating procedures, and mechanisms for collective defense—where an attack on one member is treated as an attack on all.

Multilateral alliances enhance regional or global stability by pooling military resources, deterring aggression, and enabling coordinated responses to crises or conflicts. They can vary in scope from broad, long-standing alliances like NATO to regional security pacts focused on specific threats or geographic areas.

### **Examples of Multilateral Military Alliances**

- North Atlantic Treaty Organization (NATO) – A 31-member alliance committed to collective defense in the Euro-Atlantic area.
- ANZUS Pact – A security agreement among Australia, New Zealand, and the United States.
- Collective Security Treaty Organization (CSTO) – A Russia-led defense alliance including Armenia, Belarus, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, and Tajikistan.
- Shanghai Cooperation Organization (SCO) – A political and security bloc including China, Russia, India, Pakistan, and Central Asian states, focused on regional stability and counterterrorism.
- European Union’s Common Security and Defence Policy (CSDP) – Enables EU member states to conduct joint military and crisis response operations.

These alliances are often supported by joint exercises, integrated logistics, interoperability initiatives, and long-term strategic planning.
