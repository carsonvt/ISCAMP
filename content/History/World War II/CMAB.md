## Overview

Formed shortly after the founding of the [[Combined Chiefs of Staff (CCS)]] in early 1942, the CMAB was a critical civil-military body tasked with distributing surplus Allied armaments across coalition forces. Operating under the CCS' immediate authority, CMAB  functioned through a network of sub-boards and national representatives.
## Structure & Reach

- Divided into Army, Navy, and Air Force committees
- Global distribution network including boards in Ottawa, London, Delhi, and Canberra
- Central hub in Washington, D.C.
## Inputs
- U.S. Lend-Lease Program
- Canada’s “Billion Dollar Gift” and Mutual Aid Act
- Britain’s “Reverse Lend-Lease”
## Operations

- Only surplus arms were allocated (core national needs came first)
- Assignments flowed through bureaucratic channels tied to each nation's ministries or war departments 
## Criticisms

- No executive power—decisions required national implementation
- British-Canadian–U.S. control led to limited involvement of other Allies
- Became entangled in jurisdictional rivalries, especially in the U.S.​
