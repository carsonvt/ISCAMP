## Overview

CPC was established to coordinate top-secret atomic bomb research among the three major powers. It was the most secretive and tightly controlled of the combined boards.

**Established**: August 1943, at the Québec Conference  
**Participants**: United States, United Kingdom, Canada

## Structure & Operations

- Oversaw joint research, espionage security, and uranium resource procurement
- Managed division of labor between U.S. (Manhattan Project), UK (Tube Alloys), and Canada (uranium processing)
- Handled critical technology transfers, especially in the final year of the war
## Significance

- CPC coordination enabled Anglo-American nuclear success
- Served as a foundation for the later Anglo-American nuclear partnership (e.g., Quebec Agreement)
- Early instance of science diplomacy in a coalition context​
