**Date Range**: 1942–1945  
**Participants**: United Kingdom, United States (with periodic input from Canada, China, and the Soviet Union)
## Overview

The CCS was created at the [[Arcadia Conference]] as a unified body of the U.S. Joint Chiefs of Staff and the British Chiefs of Staff. It served as an Allied strategic nerve center and principal forum for the planning and allocation of military resources. The CCS met formally at key wartime summits and worked day-to-day through a London-Washington planning mechanism.
## Significance

- First truly multinational military decision-making body of its kind
- Enabled coordination across theaters and services, particularly in Europe and the Pacific
- Precedent for post-war alliance structures (e.g., NATO Military Committee)
## Structure & Function

- Staffed by Anglo-American planners with secretariat and subcommittees for strategy, logistics, weather, communications
- Operated from Washington in peacetime; top-level coordination occurred during summits (e.g., Casablanca, Tehran)
## Challenges

- Exclusion of smaller Allies led to political tension (e.g., Australia, the Netherlands)
- Friction between U.S. Army and Navy delayed effective joint operations
- No legal enforcement powers—relied on consensus and presidential/prime ministerial backing
## Legacy
- Helped inspire the unification of American military agencies as part of the U.S. National Security Act (1947)
- Model for modern alliance coordination (e.g., Combined Joint Task Forces, NATO committees)
