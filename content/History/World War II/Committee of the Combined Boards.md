## Overview

The Committee of the Combined Boards was a short-lived but instructive effort to address coalition logistics following the Allied landings in French North Africa ([[Operation Torch]]). Convened by the U.S. State Department in December 1942, it sought to coordinate the rapid provision of food and raw materials to newly liberated territories. Though temporary, this committee marked a key transitional moment between improvised wartime collaboration and the institutionalization of international relief under UNRRA.

**Timeline**: December 1942 – January 1943  
**Convened by**: U.S. State Department  
**Participants**: U.S., U.K., and dominion representatives, drawing from various Combined Boards
## Mandate

- Coordinate provisioning of French North Africa after [[Operation Torch]]
- Serve as a clearinghouse for urgent cross-Board issues (raw materials, food, logistics)
- Act as a testbed for interdepartmental and inter-Allied relief planning
## Structure

- Informal coordination body drawing on the networks of [[Combined Munitions Assignment Board|CMAB]], [[Combined Raw Materials Board|CRMB]], and [[Combined Food Board|CFB]]
- Included senior bureaucrats with wartime collaboration experience
- No permanent personnel or enforcement powers—functioned as a forum only
## Significance

- Marked an early acknowledgment that military victory required immediate relief planning
- Seen in retrospect as a prototype for the United Nations Relief and Rehabilitation Administration (UNRRA), which absorbed many of its functions in 1943
- Reflected the growing Anglo-American recognition of the political dimensions of occupation and reconstruction
## Limitations

- Short lifespan (just over a month)
- Lacked teeth and clarity in terms of jurisdiction
- Dependent on goodwill and informal networks rather than formal authority
## Legacy

- Demonstrated the need for purpose-built international relief agencies
- Inspired aspects of UNRRA’s interagency model
- Showed that coalition logistics would increasingly include civil and humanitarian concerns alongside military objectives​
