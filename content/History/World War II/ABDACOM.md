## Overview 

The American-British-Dutch-Australian Command (ABDACOM) was the first Allied attempt at a [[Unified Command|unified multinational theater command]] in WWII. Established after the Japanese entry into the war, it covered the vast region from Burma through the Dutch East Indies to northern Australia.

**Supreme Commander**: General Sir Archibald Wavell (UK)  
**Participants**: United States, United Kingdom, Australia, the Netherlands  
**Timeline**: January 15 – February 25, 1942
## Key Functions & Intent

- Unity of command modeled on WWI precedents (e.g. Foch, Supreme War Council)
- Serve as stopgap defense against Japan in Southeast Asia
- Coordinate land, sea, and air forces under one theater command
## Challenges & Collapse

- British initially hoped a U.S. officer would take command to avoid blame for expected failures
- The U.S. insisted on Wavell, but subordinated him to the Anglo-American Combined Chiefs of Staff (CCS), leaving a vague chain of command
- National jealousies and strategic divergence undermined coordination
- Rapid Japanese advances and poor communications led to ABDACOM's disintegration within six weeks
## Legacy: 

- Demonstrated limits of early wartime coalition unity
- Lessons fed into later, more successful joint command structures (e.g. Allied Force Headquarters (AFHQ) under Eisenhower; South East Asia Command (SEAC) under Mountbatten)​
