## Overview

The CPRB was created in mid-1942 to integrate and plan the war economies of the U.S., U.K., and Canada. Supporters dubbed it a “Chiefs of Staff for Production,” hoping it would evolve into a permanent supranational institution for wartime and postwar economic management.

**Composition**
- U.S. War Production Board
- British Ministry of Production
- Canadian Department of Munitions and Supply

## Mandate

Coordinate long-range planning and allocation of resources; replace national economic planning with "combined programming"; enforce compliance and resolve disputes between national agencies. 

## Problems

CPRB met resistance from entrenched national bureaucracies, especially in the U.S. The combined board never achieved full buy-in or legal authority from the U.S. Army or Navy. 

## Impact

- Reorganized in 1943 to handle humanitarian relief to liberated areas
- Later used by UNRRA as a planning template
- Symbolic of both the ambitions and constraints of wartime internationalism​
