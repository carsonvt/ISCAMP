## Overview 

The term “United Nations High Command” was coined by _Time_ magazine in 1942 to describe the sprawling network of Allied boards and committees headquartered in Washington, D.C., which coordinated global war production, logistics, and military strategy.

**Core Components**:
- **[[Combined Chiefs of Staff (CCS)]]** – grand strategy
- **[[Combined Munitions Assignment Board (CMAB)]]** – arms allocation
- **[[Combined Raw Materials Board (CRMB)]]** – critical resources
- **[[Combined Food Board (CFB)]]** – global food distribution
- **[[Combined Shipping Adjustment Board (CSAB)]]** – maritime logistics
- **[[Combined Production and Resources Board (CPRB)]]** – integrated war economies
- **[[Combined Policy Committee (CPC)]]** – atomic research (w/ Canada)

## Criticisms
- Anglo-American exclusivity (USSR, Free French, China often sidelined)
- Unequal representation: Canada had “a ninth of a larynx” (Bottelier, "The 'Grand Alliance of Nations'" (2025))
- Bureaucratic infighting and weak legal basis limited enforcement

## Importance
- Showed how functional internationalism could work under wartime pressure
- Shaped early postwar institutional thinking (e.g., UNRRA, later [[North Atlantic Treaty Organization (NATO)|NATO]], IMF governance logic)
