## Overview

Established in 1942, CRMB was a largely civilian Anglo-American body that coordinated access to critical raw materials—like rubber, tin, and industrial minerals—needed for global war production. Unlike other Boards, it faced fewer internal political disputes and achieved a degree of functional success by acting as an international cartel for commodity access.

## Functions

- Pooled data on resource imports and stockpiles
- Organized purchases from neutrals (e.g., Argentina)
- Issued supply recommendations to national agencies
- Divided into commodity-specific subcommittees
## Distinctive Features

- Operated like a cartel: information pooling rather than directive control
- Avoided the bureaucratic paralysis seen in other Boards
- Maintained smoother coordination due to less military interference
## Challenges

- No executive power or permanent staff    
- Depended on national compliance
- Excluded USSR and lesser Allies from decision-making
## Legacy

- Set the stage for postwar resource governance structures
- Helped stabilize wartime access to scarce materials
