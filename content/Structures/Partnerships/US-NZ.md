## Overview

The U.S.–New Zealand military relationship is one of strategic cooperation without formal alliance status, a unique dynamic shaped by Cold War tensions and evolving regional security interests. While [[New Zealand]] remains part of the [[ANZUS (Australia-New Zealand-United States Security Treaty)]] (1951), its anti-nuclear stance led to a decades-long break in U.S. defense ties. Today, however, military cooperation is stronger than ever, particularly in the realms of [[Indo-Pacific]] security, intelligence-sharing under [[Five Eyes (FVEY)]], and joint military exercises.
## History

- ANZUS Treaty (1951): New Zealand joined the U.S. and Australia in a mutual defense pact, ensuring security commitments in the Pacific.
- Nuclear Fallout (1980s): New Zealand’s ban on nuclear-powered and nuclear-armed ships (1984) led to the U.S. suspending defense obligations under ANZUS.
- Post-9/11 & Military Reengagement: The U.S. and New Zealand rekindled security ties, with New Zealand supporting operations in Afghanistan and the Pacific.
- Modern [[Indo-Pacific]] Security (2010s–Present): Military cooperation resumed under the Wellington Declaration (2010) and Washington Declaration (2012), strengthening maritime security, peacekeeping, and counterterrorism efforts.
## Characteristics & Features

- Bilateral Security Agreements & Renewed Cooperation: 
   - Wellington Declaration (2010): Reaffirmed U.S.–New Zealand defense cooperation, focusing on disaster relief and regional security.
   - Washington Declaration (2012): Expanded joint military training, maritime security, and cyber defense cooperation.
- Military Exercises & Pacific Security Cooperation:
   - RIMPAC (Rim of the Pacific): New Zealand is a regular participant in the world’s largest naval exercise, led by the U.S. Navy.
   - Talisman Sabre: While not a full ANZUS partner, New Zealand conducts joint training with U.S. and Australian forces in large-scale military drills.
   - Pacific Islands Security: New Zealand works with the U.S. to counter Chinese influence in the Pacific, focusing on humanitarian assistance, peacekeeping, and disaster relief operations.
- Five Eyes Intelligence & Cybersecurity Collaboration:
   - New Zealand remains a core member of the Five Eyes intelligence network, ensuring deep intelligence-sharing and cybersecurity cooperation with the U.S.
   - The partnership includes joint counterterrorism efforts, electronic surveillance, and intelligence analysis, reinforcing regional security.
## Strategic Importance

- Indo-Pacific Stability & China’s Growing Influence: The U.S. and New Zealand are strengthening military coordination in the Pacific, addressing China’s expanding security footprint.
- Maritime & Humanitarian Security: New Zealand plays a crucial role in Pacific peacekeeping, maritime surveillance, and disaster response, ensuring regional stability.
- Intelligence & Cyber Defense Leadership: Despite past political rifts, New Zealand’s Five Eyes partnership with the U.S. remains one of its most significant security contributions.
