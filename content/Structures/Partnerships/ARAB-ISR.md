## Overview 

While Arab-Israeli military cooperation remains politically sensitive, behind closed doors, defense coordination has quietly expanded, particularly against a shared adversary: Iran. [[The Abraham Accords (2020)]] normalized ties between [[Israel]] and Gulf states like the [[UAE]] and [[Bahrain]], opening the door for intelligence-sharing, missile defense integration, and counter-drone operations. While a formal military alliance remains unlikely, Israel’s advanced defense technology and Gulf nations’ strategic locations make this an emerging security partnership to watch.

## History

- Pre-2020: Covert intelligence-sharing and backchannel diplomacy between Israel and Gulf states, particularly [[Saudi Arabia]] and the [[UAE]], existed for years due to mutual concerns over Iran and extremist groups.
- Abraham Accords (2020): The United Arab Emirates, Bahrain, Sudan, and Morocco normalized relations with Israel, leading to increased trade, security cooperation, and military coordination.
- Post-2021: Ongoing discussions between Israel and Saudi Arabia signal a potential broadening of the alliance, particularly in defense collaboration and economic integration.
- 2023-Present: Joint military exercises, missile defense coordination, and technology-sharing have increased between Israel, the UAE, Bahrain, and Egypt.
## Major Characteristics & Initiatives

- Countering Iran: The alliance focuses on countering Iranian-backed militias, ballistic missile threats, and naval disruptions in the Persian Gulf and Red Sea.
- Military Cooperation: Some Arab states participate in joint drills with Israel, particularly in air defense and maritime security.
- Economic and Technological Ties: Israel and Arab nations have expanded trade agreements, cybersecurity partnerships, and renewable energy projects.
- Intelligence Sharing: Growing cooperation on counterterrorism efforts, focusing on Iran, Hezbollah, and regional extremist groups.
- Strategic Impact: Strengthens U.S. and Western-backed security frameworks in the Middle East, shifting traditional Arab-Israeli hostilities toward pragmatic cooperation.
